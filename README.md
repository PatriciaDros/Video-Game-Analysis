# Video Games Dataset – Interactive Dashboard

In this video game data analysis project, I applied skills from my data analytics training to showcase continuous learning and development. I began by thoroughly understanding the dataset, then cleaned the data and conducted exploratory data analysis (EDA) using Python and the pandas library.

Although the dataset is commonly used in beginner projects, I elevated the experience by incorporating interactive elements to boost user engagement and stretch my technical capabilities. This process reinforced my foundational knowledge while giving me room to experiment with new features.

After analyzing the data in Python, I exported the cleaned version as a CSV and used Excel to build the dashboard. There, I created a wireframe, added a date column to display timelines, and used a timeline filter to make the dashboard interactive.

## Real-World Scenario

I like to begin every project by imagining a real-world client. In this case, I envisioned a group of recent graduates planning to develop a new video game. With so many genres and platforms already on the market, they weren’t sure how to stand out. They asked me to identify what types of games are currently successful, where the opportunities are, and the best platforms and regions for launching a game.

I translated their questions into a clear problem statement:

> **What is the ideal game genre that aligns with the most popular gaming platform and performs well in the most lucrative regions?**

## Defining the Stakeholders

With the problem and its context defined, I identified the key stakeholders—these post-grads—who would use the findings to pitch their idea to venture capitalists. Knowing the audience helped guide the format and tone of the final presentation: it needed to be data-driven, visually compelling, and accessible to both technical and non-technical viewers.

It was important to note that my role wasn’t to shape the creative direction of the game or meet VC funding requirements. Instead, my job was to use data to provide actionable insights that would support the graduates' business case.

## Measures of Success

The success of the analysis was defined by how well it answered three questions:
- Which game genres are currently most popular?
- What are the most commonly used gaming platforms?
- Which regions are the most financially viable for launching a new game?

Once we had a clear and mutually agreed-upon problem statement, the students shared the dataset and we agreed to reconvene in a few days to review the results.

## Writing an Efficient Problem Statement

Here are the guidelines I follow when crafting a strong problem statement:

1. **Define the Problem Clearly**  
   Summarize the core issue and its impact on the client or industry.

2. **Identify Stakeholders**  
   Clarify who is affected by the issue and who benefits from the solution.

3. **Set Boundaries**  
   Outline what’s within the scope and what’s not, to maintain focus.

4. **State the Objective**  
   Describe what a successful analysis will look like.

5. **Use Data**  
   Include relevant data or statistics to underscore the problem’s significance.
